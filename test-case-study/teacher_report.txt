ACADEMIC & CLASSROOM CONCERNS
================================================================================

1. FOLLOWING DIRECTIONS
Sofia demonstrates inconsistent ability to follow classroom directions. She
requires:
- Frequent repetition of multi-step directions
- Simplified language and gestural cues to understand
- 1:1 redirection more often than same-age peers
- Visual supports (pointing, demonstrating) improve compliance

Example: During circle time, when asked to "put your backpack in your cubby and
come back to the rug," Sofia looked confused. I had to repeat it, point to her
backpack, then point to the cubby, then point to the rug before she understood.

2. GROUP ACTIVITY PARTICIPATION
Sofia's participation in group activities is limited by her communication skills:
- Reluctant to participate in group discussions
- Does not volunteer answers or share ideas verbally
- Engages more in parallel activities than interactive group tasks
- Sits near the group but often watches rather than participates

3. COMMUNICATION OUTPUT IN CLASS
Sofia is notably quieter than other 4-year-olds in the class:
- Uses single words or short phrases (2-3 words) to communicate
- Heavy reliance on gestures, pointing, and nonverbal communication
- Rarely initiates verbal communication with peers
- Uses more Spanish vocabulary than English in unfamiliar contexts
- Speaks more English when discussing familiar classroom routines

4. PEER UNDERSTANDING & COMMUNICATION BREAKDOWNS
Significant communication concerns noted:
- Peers frequently ask "What?" or "I don't understand" when Sofia speaks
- Sofia becomes frustrated when not understood (visible body language, pulling away)
- Limited back-and-forth conversations with classmates
- Often resorts to nonverbal communication (pulling, pointing) when peers don't
  understand her speech

Specific incident: Sofia wanted to tell me about her new dog. She said what
sounded like "me got oggy home" (I have a doggy at home). I understood "doggy
at home" but had to ask clarifying questions. When I asked, "You have a dog?"
she said "yes" and drew a picture. However, when another child asked her about it,
Sofia couldn't explain, just said "doggy" and showed the picture.

5. SOCIAL INTERACTIONS & PLAY BEHAVIORS
Sofia's social development is slightly delayed for her age:
- Engages primarily in parallel play (playing near but not with peers)
- Demonstrates emerging cooperative play with adult support
- Does not initiate peer interactions
- Responds to peer initiations inconsistently
- Better at nonverbal play (building blocks, playing house) than verbally-based
  games (singing games, storytelling)
- When playing dolls, shows some pretend play but minimal verbal narration

6. STRENGTHS & POSITIVE ATTRIBUTES
Sofia has several notable strengths:
- Eager to please and responsive to praise
- Excellent listener in 1:1 situations; demonstrates good receptive skills
- Strong nonverbal problem-solving skills (can figure out puzzles, building tasks
  with minimal instruction)
- Persistent with challenging tasks
- Good fine motor skills (drawing, cutting, manipulating small objects)
- Demonstrates understanding of classroom routines and expectations
- Cooperative and well-behaved during transitions
- Good observer; learns by watching others
- Shows interest in learning and books

7. COMPARISON TO SAME-AGE PEERS
Sofia's communication skills are noticeably below same-age peers:
- Most 4-year-olds in my class use 4-6 word phrases
- Sofia consistently uses 2-3 word phrases
- Peers can tell coherent short stories; Sofia labels and describes but doesn't
  sequence events
- Peers engage in extended play scenarios with dialogue; Sofia engages minimally
- Peers ask "why," "how," and "when" questions regularly; Sofia asks "what" and
  "where" primarily
- Her vocabulary is smaller; peers know more color names, shape names, and
  descriptive words
- Intelligibility is noticeably lower than peers

================================================================================
TEACHER RATING SCALES (1-5, with 5 being age-appropriate)
================================================================================

EXPRESSIVE LANGUAGE SKILLS: 2
Description: Sofia uses primarily single words and 2-3 word phrases. Sentence
structure is limited. She does not use grammatical markers (plurals, past tense,
articles). Her vocabulary is smaller than same-age peers.

RECEPTIVE LANGUAGE SKILLS: 3
Description: Sofia demonstrates age-appropriate understanding of simple, concrete
directions and vocabulary in familiar contexts. She struggles with complex or
abstract language. Requires repetition and context clues.

ARTICULATION/SPEECH CLARITY: 2
Description: Sofia's speech is significantly difficult to understand in unfamiliar
contexts (~45% intelligibility). Many sound errors noted, particularly with
consonant clusters and final consonants. Even familiar communication partners
require clarification.

SOCIAL COMMUNICATION SKILLS: 2
Description: Sofia demonstrates emerging pragmatic skills. She has difficulty
with turn-taking in conversations, maintaining topics, and initiating peer
interactions. Uses more gestures than words. Limited narrative ability.

FOLLOWING DIRECTIONS: 2
Description: Sofia requires frequent repetition, simplification, and visual
supports to follow even simple directions. She does better with 1-2 step
directions than multi-step requests. Demonstrates inconsistent compliance.

================================================================================
CLASSROOM COMMUNICATION EXAMPLES
================================================================================

POSITIVE EXCHANGES:
1. During individual reading time: "Read again?" (asking for another book)
   Response: Understood, complied with request

2. During snack time: "More juice, please" (with prompting)
   Response: Understood, received juice

3. During transition: "Go outside?" (asking about next activity)
   Response: Understood, transitioned appropriately

CHALLENGING EXCHANGES:
1. Sofia said something that sounded like "i want poon" during art. After
   questioning, she pointed to the spoon (poon = spoon due to cluster reduction
   and final consonant deletion). Peer responded "What?" and Sofia got frustrated.

2. During storytelling circle, Sofia tried to share about her brother but said
   something like "my brer sicky" (my brother is sick). I understood from context
   and nodding confirmation, but other children looked confused.

3. Sofia said "that one mine" (that one is mine) during toy selection. Correct
   meaning understood, but grammatically simplified compared to same-age peers
   who would say "that one is mine" or "that's the one I want."

4. When frustrated with a puzzle, Sofia said "no do it" instead of "I can't do
   it" or "I need help."

ADDITIONAL OBSERVATIONS
================================================================================

ENGAGEMENT IN CLASSROOM:
Sofia is an engaged learner when activities are visually interesting and/or
hands-on. She focuses well during fine motor activities (drawing, playdough,
puzzles) but loses interest during verbally-intensive activities (long group
discussions, storytime without pictures).

CONFIDENCE & BEHAVIOR:
Sofia is generally well-behaved and well-liked by peers, despite communication
challenges. However, I have noticed she becomes withdrawn in group situations
where she cannot communicate effectively. She withdraws from large group
conversations and prefers 1:1 interactions.

ATTENDANCE & CONSISTENCY:
Sofia attends regularly (excellent attendance record) and is consistent in her
communication patterns across days.

PREVIOUS INTERVENTIONS:
No previous speech/language services at our school. This is the first formal
referral for evaluation.

================================================================================
TEACHER'S RECOMMENDATION
================================================================================

I recommend Sofia receive a comprehensive speech-language evaluation. Her
expressive language and articulation skills are significantly below same-age
peers, and this is impacting her social interactions and classroom participation.
While she is clearly intelligent and understands more than she can express, her
communication difficulties are beginning to affect her confidence and peer
relationships.

I would welcome speech-language services to support her development.
