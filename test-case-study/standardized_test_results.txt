PHONOLOGICAL PROCESS ANALYSIS
================================================================================

A systematic analysis of Sofia's speech sample reveals the following phonological
processes:

PROCESS 1: CLUSTER REDUCTION (85% occurrence)
Description: Sofia reduces consonant clusters to single consonants
Examples from speech sample:
- spoon → /pun/ (poon)
- tree → /ti/ (tee)
- bring → /bɪŋ/ (bing instead of bring)
- try → /taɪ/ (try - not reduced, but marked as emerging pattern)

Typical in: Ages 2-3 years
Concern at age 4;4: Persistent - should be suppressed

Strategy Used: Usually deletes the first member of cluster

PROCESS 2: FRONTING OF VELAR CONSONANTS (70% occurrence)
Description: Sofia produces alveolar /t, d/ for velar /k, g/
Examples from speech sample:
- cat → /tæt/ (tat)
- go → /do/ (do)
- cookie → /tuti/ (tutty or tutee)
- dog → /dɑg/ (dog - correct, emerging suppression)

Typical in: Ages 2.5-4 years
Status at age 4;4: Moderately persistent

PROCESS 3: GLIDING (90% occurrence - most prominent process)
Description: Sofia substitutes glides /w, j/ for liquids /r, l/
Examples from speech sample:
- rabbit → /wæbɪt/ (wabbit)
- run → /wʌn/ (wun)
- like → /waɪk/ (wike)
- red → /wɛd/ (wed)
- light → /waɪt/ (wite)
- dog → /dɑg/ (correct - emerging)
- little → /lɪtəl/ (little - correct production, minimal)

Typical in: Ages 3-6 years (longer developmental window)
Status at age 4;4: Persistent and most frequent process

PROCESS 4: STOPPING OF FRICATIVES (40% occurrence - showing suppression)
Description: Sofia substitutes stops for fricative consonants
Examples from speech sample:
- fish → /fɪʃ/ (fish - correct, emerging suppression)
- sun → /sʌn/ (sun - correct)
- fun → /fʌn/ (fun - correct)
- soup → /sup/ (correct production noted)
- five → /faɪv/ (correct, emerging)
- sit → /sɪt/ (correct)
- shoe → /ʃu/ (correct)

Earlier examples (showing suppression emerging):
- said /f/ → /p/ (p'aid)
- soap /s/ → /p/ (poap)

Typical in: Ages 2-4 years
Status at age 4;4: Beginning to suppress - positive prognostic indicator

ADDITIONAL PHONOLOGICAL PROCESSES NOTED:

FINAL CONSONANT DELETION (60% occurrence)
Description: Sofia frequently omits final consonants
Examples:
- dog → /dɑ/ (da)
- sit → /sɪ/ (si)
- cup → /kʌ/ (cu or cuh)

Typical in: Ages 2-3.5 years
Status at age 4;4: Should be suppressed; persistent

STATUS: Minimal, age-appropriate

CLINICAL INTERPRETATION & SUMMARY
================================================================================

Sofia Martinez presents with significant speech-language delays affecting both
expressive language and articulation. Her receptive language is relatively
stronger than her expressive language, indicating that her understanding exceeds
her ability to produce language.

KEY FINDINGS:

1. EXPRESSIVE LANGUAGE DISORDER: Sofia demonstrates a Very Low expressive
   language composite (Standard Score 73, 4th percentile) affecting:
   - Sentence production (difficulty with grammatical forms)
   - Vocabulary access (limited expressive vocabulary)
   - Morphosyntax (missing grammatical markers)

2. PHONOLOGICAL DISORDER: Sofia demonstrates a Very Low articulation score
   (Standard Score 68, 2nd percentile) characterized by:
   - Persistent gliding of liquids
   - Cluster reduction
   - Final consonant deletion
   - Fronting of velars (emerging suppression)
   - Reduced intelligibility (~65% in familiar context, ~45% in unfamiliar)

3. RECEPTIVE-EXPRESSIVE DISCREPANCY: Sofia's receptive language is relatively
   stronger than expressive language:
   - RLI: 80 (9th percentile)
   - ELI: 73 (4th percentile)
   This indicates better understanding than production ability.

4. BILINGUAL CONSIDERATIONS: Sofia is a bilingual Spanish-English learner. While
   bilingual exposure does not cause language disorders, multilingualism should
   be considered in interpretation of results. Sofia may be distributing her
   vocabulary across two languages, but her overall communication across both
   languages is significantly below expectations.

DIAGNOSTIC IMPRESSION:

Sofia Martinez meets criteria for:
- EXPRESSIVE LANGUAGE DISORDER (phonological and syntactic subtypes)
- PHONOLOGICAL DISORDER / SPEECH SOUND DISORDER

These are not attributable to hearing loss, oral mechanism abnormality, or
neurological disorder. Language delay is the primary concern.
