SPEECH & LANGUAGE DEVELOPMENT HISTORY
================================================================================

FIRST WORDS:
Sofia said her first words at around 18 months of age. This was about normal
timing - her older brother was also 18 months when he first started talking.
However, Maria notes that "Sofia didn't seem to say many new words after that
first one."

Her first words included:
- "mamá" (mama)
- "papá" (papa)
- "agua" (water)
- "no" (no)

VOCABULARY GROWTH:
By age 2 years, Sofia was using about 30-40 words, mostly in Spanish. Maria says
her siblings had "more words by this age, but I thought every kid was different."
By age 3, Sofia was using about 75-100 words combined (Spanish and English).

At age 3.5, when Sofia started preschool, she began picking up more English but
hasn't really "exploded" with language growth. Maria is concerned because her
older daughter at this age was speaking in longer sentences.

WORD COMBINATIONS & SENTENCES:
Sofia started combining two words around 30 months of age. Maria notes this was
"later than my other kids - my son was combining words closer to 24 months, and
my daughter was similar."

Early combinations included:
- "más agua" (more water)
- "mi mamá" (my mommy)
- "no quiero" (I don't want)

Currently, Sofia mostly uses 2-3 word combinations. Maria says she understands
that Sofia is bilingual and that might slow things down, "but I'm still worried
because she says simple things like 'me want dat' instead of 'I want that.'"

SPEECH CLARITY:
Maria is most concerned about Sofia's speech clarity. She says, "People don't
understand her. My neighbor asked me to translate what Sofia was saying. Even my
mother, who watches her sometimes, asks me 'what did she say?'"

At home, Maria understands about 85% of what Sofia says (because of context and
familiarity), but when Sofia tries to talk to others, they often ask "what?" or
"say it again?" Sofia gets frustrated and "shuts down" or repeats even less
clearly.

Specific example: Sofia was trying to tell her teacher about a family trip to the
zoo. She said something like "we see-ed monkies" and her teacher asked three times
before understanding. Sofia gave up and didn't finish her story. Maria says Sofia
seems embarrassed when people don't understand her.

FAMILY HISTORY OF SPEECH/LANGUAGE DISORDERS:
Sofia's older brother (age 7) received speech therapy when he was 3 years old. He
had similar concerns - "his speech was hard to understand." He went to speech
therapy for about 1.5 years and "now he talks great and no one has any problem
understanding him." He's doing well in first grade.

No other family members have had speech/language issues. Maria's father had a
hearing problem in older age, but that's unrelated.

================================================================================
CURRENT CONCERNS - WHAT WORRIES PARENTS MOST
================================================================================

PRIMARY CONCERN - SPEECH INTELLIGIBILITY:
"The biggest worry is that people can't understand Sofia. I worry about when she
starts real school - will the teacher understand her? Will other kids make fun of
her because they can't understand what she's saying? My older son went through
this for a while and it made him shy. I don't want Sofia to be shy or feel bad
about herself."

SECONDARY CONCERN - LANGUAGE SKILLS:
"Sofia doesn't say things correctly. She should be saying longer sentences by
now. She says 'me want dat' when she should say 'I want that.' And she says
'doggy go' when she should say 'the doggy is going.'"

SOCIAL & EMOTIONAL CONCERN:
"Sofia gets frustrated when people don't understand her. She'll stamp her feet or
say 'no!' and just turn away. Then she won't try to talk anymore. I'm worried
about her confidence."

OTHER CONCERNS:
"Sometimes I worry that maybe her hearing is bad? But we did the test at the
pediatrician and it was normal. Maybe it's just because she's learning two
languages? But my other kids learned two languages and they're speaking fine."
